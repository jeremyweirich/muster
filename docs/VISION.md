# Common Ground — Vision

The owner's vision for the game's story and themes, kept close to their own words (it started as `PROMPT.md`, Oct
2026). The Kingdom has since been renamed the **Wardens**; this file uses the new name. `docs/CAMPAIGN.md` turns it into
campaigns and missions, and DESIGN.md stays the source of truth for mechanics. When the two disagree on tone or story,
this file wins.

## The narrative thread

The battlefield is a game that all three peoples agreed to play.

Generations ago, the three peoples fought an open war that nearly wrecked the world: Warden walls against goblin
swarms against elemental storms. It ended in a treaty, the Compact. Now disputes over land, insults and old grudges are
settled on the Field. Each commander chooses champions and places them. Once the horn sounds, no one may touch the
fight. The champions really die, and the stakes are real. But the rules are the same for everyone.

This framing does a lot of work:
- **It explains the autobattler in-world.** You prepare, then you let go. The hands-off rule is the Compact's most
  sacred law, so tutorial text can be written as the rules of the Compact.
- **It makes "same game, balanced" literal.** Each faction privately believes the Compact favors the others and that
  its own way is the correct way to play. The campaigns prove that all three are wrong.
- **The battles stay vicious.** It's like siblings who fight to the death over a board game, except someone actually
  dies.

## The triangle

Each campaign ends by exploiting the next faction's weakness, and the following campaign fixes it. This is a story
structure: missions build these edges with authored armies. Skirmish balance is per unit and doesn't follow the
triangle (`docs/BALANCE.md`).

- **Wardens beat Goblins.** Discipline crushes chaos, because the goblins have no answer to the pike line.
- **Goblins beat Elementals.** The goblins invent an answer and get revenge on the Wardens. Then they find out that
  elemental fireballs hurt elementals too, and they start steering the fire.
- **Elementals beat Wardens.** A tight shield wall is the perfect target for a water-and-lightning combo field.
- **Epilogue:** the Wardens, steady as ever, add a new page to their drill book: "Against storms, spread out."

The final standings are tied. Nobody won, and everybody got better. The last scene: the three commanders at the
scoreboard. "Same time next season?" The Goblin says yes immediately, the Warden says yes after checking the calendar,
and the Elemental pauses, then says yes.

Structurally, each campaign can open by replaying the previous campaign's final battle from the losing side. That's
very Warcraft 3, and it teaches the counter by showing the player exactly why they lost.

## The deeper thread

Each faction's weakness is the flip side of its strength, and each strategy is a different way of taking care of your
own:

- **Wardens** handle risk through preparation.
- **Goblins** handle it through numbers and each other. They're reckless together, and the gang absorbs the losses.
- **Elementals** handle it through precision, because they're dangerous to their friends.

All three are forms of care that look nothing alike from the outside. That's the quiet message under the loud battles.

## Faction voices

### Wardens: proud of being boring

This is the small dose of personality, and it reinforces their blandness instead of fighting it. Their tutorial device
is the **Drill Book**, a centuries-old manual of formations, with one page per mission.

- The player's commander is a newly appointed **Marshal**, a little anxious and comforted by the book. Their onboarding
  mirrors the player's.
- A **dwarf sergeant**: "Boring wins. Write that down."
- An **elf archivist** notes that this formation hasn't changed in four hundred years. "And it has never once needed to."
- **Arc:** the Marshal learns the book can grow. The last page is the first one they write themselves.

### Goblins: chaos with a lot of heart

Two leads: **the Boss** and **the Tinker**.

- The campaign opens with the replayed Warden finale. "Lads, they had pointy sticks and we had nothing. So I made a
  bigger thing."
- The Tinker announces the odds on every gadget and always gets them wrong: "One in three chance it works! ...or one in
  three it doesn't. One of those." That's the risk/reward tutorial voice.
- The fireball mission: "Fire's hot. Fire's also free. Point it at THEM." Goblins who go out in a blaze get cheered,
  not mourned.

### Elementals: not arrogant, careful

The goblin campaign shows them as aloof snobs. Their own campaign reveals the truth.

- A **young fire elemental** is afraid of burning its neighbors. Positioning, the hardest skill in the game, becomes an
  act of care.
- An **elder**: "We are not complicated because we are clever. We are complicated because we burn."
- **Payoff:** when the combos land perfectly, it's the most beautiful thing on the board. Hard to do, and worth it.
